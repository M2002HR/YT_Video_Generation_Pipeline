# Main Character Anchor — The World Behind the Question

## Purpose

Generate the canonical identity reference for the recurring protagonist. This is not a scene, not a character sheet, and not a finished video frame. The selected result will become the source of truth for the character across the lifetime of the channel.

## Generation prompt

Create exactly ONE original full-body 2D cartoon character illustration of a recurring young male protagonist for a long-running educational adventure and storytelling universe.

He is a young adult, approximately 20–24 years old: highly intelligent, relentlessly curious, inventive, warm, friendly, slightly eccentric, and brave enough to follow a difficult question wherever it leads. He should immediately read as someone who notices details, connects clues, experiments with ideas, opens strange books, and genuinely enjoys discovering how the world works.

### Art direction

Use an original late-20th-century hand-drawn educational-adventure cartoon language: traditional 2D animation, lively hand-inked outlines, slightly imperfect line weight, readable shapes, expressive posing, cel-like color blocks, restrained two-step shading, and a faint gouache/watercolor or painted-cel texture.

The result should feel like a beautifully designed frame from an old hand-drawn educational adventure series while remaining an entirely original character and design language.

Keep the rendering graphic and illustrative:
- no photorealism
- no semi-realistic fantasy concept art
- no 3D rendering
- no glossy modern animation finish
- no anime rendering
- no hyper-detailed skin, hair, leather, or fabric
- no dramatic realistic movie lighting

### Face and hair

Give him an original youthful face with simplified, expressive cartoon construction.

His eyes should be alert and bright, with strong expressive eyebrows that make curiosity readable even at thumbnail size.

Hair:
- chestnut to dark-brown
- medium-short
- softly wavy
- slightly asymmetrical
- one loose front curl that consistently leans toward one side
- intentionally imperfect rather than fashion-styled

The hair silhouette should be recognizable without becoming exaggerated.

### Signature glasses

He wears small, thin, warm-brass spectacles with softly rounded, slightly oval lenses.

They are a subtle trademark, not a costume gimmick:
- relatively small on his face
- delicate wire frame
- slightly imperfect hand-drawn circularity
- understated warm-metal accent
- never oversized

### Proportions and body language

Use youthful, agile cartoon proportions: slim-to-average build, slightly long limbs, readable hands, active posture.

He should look like someone who moves, climbs ladders, reaches for books, crawls through strange spaces, and gets excited when he discovers a clue.

Avoid superhero anatomy and avoid childlike proportions.

### Core outfit

Design a simple, iconic explorer-scholar outfit:
- short deep-teal / muted moss-green overshirt or lightweight coat
- warm cream collarless shirt
- dark charcoal trousers, casually rolled once near the ankle
- sturdy simple brown lace-up boots
- compact weathered crossbody satchel

The outfit should feel timeless and practical rather than modern streetwear or historical costume.

Use simplified shapes and only enough seams/pockets to make the design memorable.

### Small unique identity details

Add a few restrained recurring details:
- sleeves casually rolled to the forearm
- warm amber inner lining visible only in small places
- one slightly mismatched repair stitch on a coat cuff
- two or three tiny paper tabs clipped into the satchel strap
- a small brass fastener on the satchel that echoes the glasses
- one subtle stitched discovery/compass motif on the inside edge of the coat, visible only when the pose allows

These details should reward repeat viewing without making the design noisy.

### Signature object — the Page Key

He carries a small original object called the Page Key.

The Page Key is a thin handcrafted brass artifact, approximately bookmark-sized, combining the visual logic of a page marker, a tiny mechanical key, and a simple navigational instrument.

It has a few clean engraved geometric lines and one small amber enamel inset.

For this canonical character reference, keep it mostly non-magical: only the faintest warm glint, no large glow, no energy beam, no wand-like appearance.

It may hang from or be held near the satchel.

### Pose and expression

Show exactly one character in a relaxed but lively three-quarter full-body pose.

He has just noticed something interesting just outside the frame.

Expression:
- eyes focused with genuine curiosity
- eyebrows slightly raised
- a small asymmetrical half-smile
- thoughtful excitement rather than exaggerated surprise

One hand may lightly hold or inspect the Page Key; the other can rest near the satchel or make a small natural thinking gesture.

### Palette

Core identity palette:
- deep teal / muted moss green
- warm cream
- charcoal
- weathered brown
- small brass/amber accents

Keep the palette appealing and easy to recognize in both bright and dark subject worlds.

### Background

Use a very simple warm parchment / muted cream painted background with subtle paper or gouache texture.

No library.
No bookshelves.
No portal.
No magical room.
No other characters.

The purpose of this image is to lock the protagonist, not the environment.

## Long-term continuity rule

This character will travel into many different worlds.

Future clothing may adapt lightly to a subject world, but the adaptation should normally affect only 15–30% of the design.

Examples:
- Ancient Egypt: locally influenced collar, linen texture, or small accessory; not a full pharaoh costume.
- Ancient Greece: restrained draped fabric accent; keep the core silhouette.
- Space/science: a few functional futuristic details; not a complete anonymous spacesuit unless the story absolutely requires one.
- Medieval history: subtle material/fastener changes; do not turn him into a knight.

Always preserve:
- face identity
- asymmetrical wavy hair and front curl
- small rounded brass glasses
- recognizable slim silhouette
- satchel or a clearly evolved equivalent
- Page Key
- teal/moss + warm accent identity where reasonably possible

The audience should recognize him instantly even when the world around him changes.

## Hard constraints

- exactly one original character
- exactly one image
- full body
- no character sheet
- no multiple poses
- no multiple panels
- no labels or text
- no school uniform
- no wizard robe
- no wand
- no superhero costume
- no direct resemblance to any existing famous character, franchise, or copyrighted character design
