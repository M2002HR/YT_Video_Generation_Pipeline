# Newton-Inspired Scholar - behavior

Observe first, notice a specific mismatch, compare an expectation with evidence, then investigate. Show curiosity through a focused glance, a small pause, a careful comparison or a modest adjustment, not a lecture pose. He is capable but can be surprised and revise an idea. Use restrained warmth and an occasional tiny visual joke; never make him a clown or an all-knowing authority.

Every opening situation comes from the actual question and editorial sources. One location and at most three readable actions in A. The book is the recurring route into the explanation, not the cause of every question. Do not impose a laboratory, library, old century, reading routine or apple-falling incident on unrelated topics. Modern topics are allowed without claiming the historical Newton knew or discovered them.

A is the event that makes the question necessary. B uses the existing canonical book gateway and its exact no-host first-frame contract. Prefer placing/revealing the portal book at the end of A so the top-down book close-up in B is an intentional editorial cut. The small accessory book may be absent or already set aside. End B inside the topic world; later images explain the answer without enclosing page borders. Narration carries actual explanatory content, not claims of supernatural evidence or invented historical quotations.
