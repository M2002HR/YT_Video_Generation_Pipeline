# International spoken English - language policy v1

Write for international teens and adults who use English as an additional language. Aim for
B1 comprehension using familiar everyday words, not a claimed CEFR certification. Keep the
subject interesting and the tone adult; simplify the language, not the facts or the viewer.

- Prefer the common meaning of common words, concrete people/actions and active sentences.
  One clear thought at a time; make pronouns, comparisons and cause/effect easy to follow once.
  Clarity comes before compression: a slightly longer familiar phrase beats one formal word.
- Avoid unnecessary academic wording, dense abstract noun phrases, obscure idioms, regional
  slang and decorative metaphors. Natural contractions are fine. Do not use baby talk.
- Explain an unfamiliar idea in plain words before or alongside its necessary technical name.
  Keep essential names and terms, repeating the same clear label instead of rotating synonyms.
  Do not ban words by length, penalize names, or add definitions for already familiar words.
- Preserve facts, numbers, units, scope, negation and uncertainty (may/can/often/some). Never turn
  a possibility into certainty, erase an important distinction, or invent an explanatory fact.
- The hook, entry clue, body, closing and CTA all follow this policy. Use the actual scene or
  a concrete comparison, not vague 'social meaning' language. Ask a specific, easy CTA question.
- Preserve the selected story, character tone, narration keys and visual-beat contract. A richer
  visual-direction vocabulary is allowed: this policy targets ONLY words the viewer hears.

Examples of direction, NOT a phrase blacklist or reusable script:
'differs from' -> 'is different from'; 'signal concealment' -> say who expects whom not to look;
'exact mechanism remains unsettled' -> 'scientists still do not know exactly how it works'.
Do not 'improve' simple wording into formal synonyms during editing. Apply the policy even when
the audience field is blank; an audience description is not a request for difficult English.
