## Description — paste into YouTube

Why can a tiny luxury survive when money gets tight? The lipstick effect describes how tighter budgets can push bigger indulgences aside while a smaller comfort such as lipstick still feels affordable. Juliet Schor described this behavior in 1998, and Leonard Lauder popularized the “lipstick index” in 2001, suggesting lipstick sales might rise when the economy weakens. Later downturns did not repeat that pattern reliably, so lipstick sales are not a dependable economic indicator. “Lipstick syndrome” is also not a recognized medical diagnosis; here it is an informal label for small comforts under pressure.
