# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
It’s often called the lipstick effect.

Visual:
Ink-wash editorial still of a single lipstick tube standing upright beside a nearly empty coin purse on warm ivory paper, the lipstick-red tip acting as the only vivid accent while muted charcoal shadows suggest financial strain.

### Beat 2

Narration:
Tight budgets can push bigger luxuries aside.

Visual:
Ink-wash scene of a hand sliding a large elegant shopping bag and a luxury shoe box away from the foreground while a thin wallet with only a few coins remains near the center, rendered in recession-gray taupe and charcoal.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Lipstick can still feel like affordable comfort.

Visual:
Ink-wash close-up of an ordinary shopper's hand selecting one modest lipstick from a sparse cosmetic counter while larger luxury items blur into pale gray wash behind it; the small red cosmetic feels warm and attainable.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Juliet Schor described this behavior in 1998.

Visual:
Late-1990s editorial research scene in ink wash: a female sociologist figure at a wooden desk studying consumer notes and books beside a small lipstick, with period-appropriate clothing, paper files, and an older desktop computer shape kept free of readable text.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Leonard Lauder popularized the lipstick index in 2001.

Visual:
Early-2000s department-store cosmetics floor rendered in soft ink wash, with a suited male executive observing rows of lipstick displays from the aisle; period packaging silhouettes and restrained red accents evoke the era without any readable branding.

### Beat 6

Narration:
He suggested lipstick sales might rise when the economy weakens.

Visual:
Editorial ink-wash metaphor showing a gray descending economic slope on one side while several lipstick tubes rise upward like simple physical markers on the other, with no numbers, labels, arrows, or chart text.

### Beat 7

Narration:
The idea became a quirky recession signal.

Visual:
Ink-wash metaphor of a lipstick tube perched like the needle of an old-fashioned economic gauge beside a small storm cloud over a miniature shopping street, playful but restrained and entirely free of labels or numbers.

### Beat 8

Narration:
Later downturns did not repeat the pattern reliably.

Visual:
Ink-wash tabletop with several recession-era shopping moments represented by scattered lipstick tubes pointing in inconsistent directions among faded receipts, coins, and closed wallets, creating an irregular pattern rather than a clean trend.

### Beat 9

Narration:
So lipstick sales are not a dependable economic indicator.

Visual:
Editorial ink-wash image of a lipstick balanced precariously on a tilted brass scale opposite a cluster of mixed economic objects such as groceries, fuel canister silhouette, coins, and a house key, emphasizing that one cosmetic cannot reliably represent the whole economy.

### Beat 10

Narration:
And “lipstick syndrome” is not a recognized medical diagnosis.

Visual:
Ink-wash still of a lipstick resting outside a closed doctor's medical bag and stethoscope, clearly separated from the clinical objects by open ivory space; the cosmetic remains vivid red while the medical items stay subdued charcoal and taupe.
