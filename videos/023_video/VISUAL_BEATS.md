# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
It's called the lipstick effect.

Visual:
In the ink-wash editorial world, a single lipstick tube stands beside a nearly empty coin purse on a modest home table, with the protagonist leaning in from the opposite side as if recognizing the connection.

### Beat 2

Narration:
Some call it lipstick syndrome.

Visual:
A close overhead ink-wash still life shows a lipstick tube casting a soft shadow shaped like a small recurring ripple beside scattered coins, suggesting the alternate name without any lettering.

### Beat 3

Narration:
The theory starts with tighter budgets.

Visual:
The protagonist sits at a small kitchen table tightening the drawstring of a coin purse while a few coins and a blank-lined shopping slip sit nearby.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Big luxuries get cut.

Visual:
An unlabeled balance-like economic diagram shows several large luxury silhouettes sinking out of the illustration window while one tiny lipstick remains above the cutoff line.

### Beat 5

Narration:
That new car waits.

Visual:
Through a modest home window, the protagonist looks toward a distant muted car parked behind a closed gate, while the coin purse rests untouched on the interior sill.

### Beat 6

Narration:
The holiday gets postponed.

Visual:
A closed travel suitcase sits pushed beneath a hallway bench, gathering a faint wash of dust while the protagonist walks past carrying groceries instead.

### Beat 7

Narration:
But lipstick may still feel affordable.

Visual:
At a modest cosmetics counter, a hand holds one small lipstick above a few remaining coins, with larger blurred products receding into muted gray wash behind it.

### Beat 8

Narration:
It's a small, feel-good purchase.

Visual:
The protagonist stands before a small mirror at home, holding the newly purchased lipstick near the chest and showing a subtle relieved smile rather than a glamorous pose.

### Beat 9

Narration:
After the 2001 downturn, Leonard Lauder noticed stronger lipstick sales.

Visual:
A restrained recession-era cosmetics counter is shown from above with many lipstick tubes being gathered into a sales tray while other product spaces remain sparse, using quantity alone to imply stronger sales.

### Beat 10

Narration:
He called it the lipstick index.

Visual:
A simple ink-wash economic metaphor shows a single lipstick tube acting like the needle of an unlabeled gauge, angled upward over a semicircular charcoal scale with no marks or numbers.

### Beat 11

Narration:
Later research found mixed results.

Visual:
Two uneven pools of ink spread in opposite directions around a lipstick placed between them, one wash rising and the other fading, conveying conflicting findings without charts or labels.

### Beat 12

Narration:
Some found higher cosmetics spending among certain groups during recessions.

Visual:
A small group of differently styled shoppers at an understated cosmetics counter each reaches toward modest beauty items while muted recession-gray storefront shapes sit beyond the window.

### Beat 13

Narration:
Others found lipstick wasn't a reliable economic signal.

Visual:
A lipstick stands beside a wavering, broken charcoal trend line that changes direction repeatedly, while a steady household coin stack sits separately in the foreground.

### Beat 14

Narration:
It's a theory about consumer psychology.

Visual:
The protagonist sits quietly at the home table between a closed coin purse and a lipstick, one hand near each, looking thoughtfully from necessity to comfort as soft ink-wash shadows merge beneath the objects.
