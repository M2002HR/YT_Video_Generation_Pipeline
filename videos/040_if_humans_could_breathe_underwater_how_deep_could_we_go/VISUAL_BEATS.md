# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Body tissue with lots of water barely changes.

Visual:
Full-bleed painted gouache underwater close-up of a human torso in translucent anatomical cutaway, with water-rich muscle and organs retaining their normal shape while deep marine water presses in from all sides; matte indigo and teal glazing, bone-white anatomy accents, no text or borders.

### Beat 2

Narration:
Air in your ears and sinuses shrinks much more.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache profile cutaway of a submerged human head, enlarged ear canal and sinus cavities glowing restrained coral-orange as their air pockets visibly contract inward, surrounded by muted aqua tissue and dark indigo water; no labels, panels, or printed symbols.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Every ten meters, pressure rises by about the air pressure at the surface.

Visual:
Full-bleed vertical gouache water column seen from a distant side angle, with one small descending diver silhouette passing successive evenly spaced depth zones; each lower zone is rendered with progressively denser translucent indigo pressure glazing and dimmer top light, without numbers, gauges, or text.

### Beat 4

Narration:
At ten meters, total pressure is about double.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache underwater scene at a single shallow-depth ledge, viewed slightly upward: a diver hovers beside two visually matched translucent pressure halos, one pale surface-reference halo above and a second added water-pressure halo surrounding the diver, together conveying doubled total pressure without any text, numbers, or gauge faces.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Unequal pressure can injure your ears and sinuses.

Visual:
Full-bleed frontal three-quarter gouache head cutaway underwater, with one ear space and the sinus region compressed unevenly against surrounding tissue; restrained coral-orange blooms mark stressed membranes while the rest of the face remains cool aqua and bone-white, avoiding gore and readable labels.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Breathing underwater removes the problem of running out of air.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache medium shot of a generic submerged diver calmly breathing through underwater breathing equipment, chest expanded naturally as a clean stream of exhaled bubbles rises toward pale surface light; composition emphasizes successful breathing rather than depth, with no gauges or text.

### Beat 7

Narration:
But pressure would still affect your body.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache wide shot from below of the same generic diver deeper in the water column, still breathing normally while broad translucent pressure gradients close around the whole body and small coral ear-cavity highlights remain compressed; the surface is now distant and dim.

### Beat 8

Narration:
At extreme depths, high pressure can disturb how nerves normally work.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache anatomical cutaway of a submerged upper body at extreme depth, viewed obliquely from behind, with the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves rendered as pale violet pathways whose normally smooth glow breaks into irregular scattered pulses under dense indigo pressure glazing; no text or mechanical readouts.

### Beat 9

Narration:
So breathing alone does not set a fixed depth limit.

Visual:
Full-bleed gouache long vertical composition showing a small breathing diver suspended between brighter water above and an open, increasingly dark abyss below, with no hard barrier, depth line, gauge, or endpoint; the diver still exhales bubbles while multiple subtle pressure effects intensify gradually with depth.

### Beat 10

Narration:
Breathing solves one limit, but pressure creates others.

Visual:
Full-bleed painted gouache final underwater tableau: a generic diver breathes comfortably in the upper third while, deeper below, the surrounding world reveals three distinct pressure consequences in one unified scene—compressed ear and sinus air spaces, largely unchanged water-rich tissue, and faint violet nerve disruption—arranged through depth rather than panels, labels, or borders.
