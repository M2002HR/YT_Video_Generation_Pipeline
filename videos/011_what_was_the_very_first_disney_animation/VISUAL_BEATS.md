# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Disney's earliest Kansas City cartoons were the Laugh-O-Grams.

Visual:
A small early-1920s Kansas City animation workspace rendered as a vertical retro educational illustration: wooden drawing desk, pinned animation sheets showing simple fairy-tale-style cartoon figures, ink bottle, pencils, registration pegs, and a modest film camera stand, all on warm aged paper with loose sepia ink contours and restrained wash; no readable lettering.

### Beat 2

Narration:
They came years before Mickey.

Visual:
A vertical visual timeline metaphor without labels: at the lower foreground, rough early cartoon animation sheets and a hand-cranked film reel; farther upward and noticeably separated in time, only a distant generic round-eared mouse-like silhouette is hinted at behind several intervening calendar-page shapes, kept abstract and secondary so it does not imply the mouse came first.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Next came the Alice Comedies.

Visual:
An early-1920s film set and drawing table sharing one simple vertical composition: a young live-action girl figure suggested in period dress near a plain camera setup, with loose hand-drawn cartoon animals emerging from animation sheets beside her; warm cream paper, sepia ink, muted ochre and faded teal accents, no text.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Those blended live action with animation.

Visual:
A single integrated scene showing a period live-action girl standing on a simple photographed floor while hand-drawn ink characters and sketched props appear to interact around her, with the boundary between filmed reality and drawn animation visibly merging through loose ink strokes rather than split panels.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Steamboat Willie was a milestone, not the start.

Visual:
A chronological depth composition in the same 1920s ink style: foreground contains earlier worn animation sheets, ink bottles, and film reels; farther along the visual path sits a later simple river-steamboat cinema motif illuminated like an important milestone, but clearly positioned after the earlier materials, with no branded characters or readable title text.
