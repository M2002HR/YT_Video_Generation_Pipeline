Could this jammed gear belong to an ancient Greek computer? I pull out the storybook, open it, and bronze wheels appear. In 1901, divers found a wreck near Antikythera. A corroded lump hid bronze gears. The mechanism was about two millennia old. A hand crank turned interlocking wheels. Pointers tracked the Sun and Moon. Other gears displayed the Moon’s changing phase. Rear dials counted calendar cycles. A spiral dial predicted eclipses. Another marked major games, including the Olympics. Researchers reconstructed its hidden gearwork. Its complexity inspired the nickname ancient computer. It modeled the sky without electricity. A shipwreck preserved astronomy built into gears.
