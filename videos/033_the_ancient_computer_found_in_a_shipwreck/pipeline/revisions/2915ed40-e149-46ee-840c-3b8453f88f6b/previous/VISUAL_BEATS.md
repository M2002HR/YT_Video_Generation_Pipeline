# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
In 1901, divers found a wreck near Antikythera.

Visual:
Vertical layered paper-cut underwater scene: two period divers descend toward a broken ancient ship resting on a rocky Mediterranean seabed, with amphorae and timber fragments emerging from cool slate-blue water.

### Beat 2

Narration:
A corroded lump hid bronze gears.

Visual:
Close paper-cut archaeological reveal of a diver's gloved hands lifting an irregular green-brown corroded bronze mass, with one small honey-amber gear edge visible through a cracked encrusted surface.

### Beat 3

Narration:
The mechanism was about two millennia old.

Visual:
Paper-cut time metaphor showing the corroded mechanism fragment in the foreground while a ghosted Hellenistic Greek ship and ancient coastal silhouette appear far behind it in warm cream and muted olive layers.

### Beat 4

Narration:
A hand crank turned interlocking wheels.

Visual:
Cutaway paper reconstruction of the mechanism viewed three-quarter close, with a simple side crank being turned by the Red Horned Everyman and several bronze gear wheels visibly meshing inside the case.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Pointers tracked the Sun and Moon.

Visual:
Front-facing paper-cut dial with two distinct pointer arms sweeping across a circular celestial face, one ending in a restrained amber Sun disc and the other in a pale cream Moon disc.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Other gears displayed the Moon’s changing phase.

Visual:
Paper-cut close-up of a small two-tone lunar sphere mounted on the mechanism, half cream and half slate, with compact bronze gears beneath indicating the sphere's rotation from bright to dark.

### Beat 7

Narration:
Rear dials counted calendar cycles.

Visual:
Rear view of the reconstructed mechanism in paper cut, dominated by broad nested circular and spiral tracks with a slender indicator arm moving through repeated calendar divisions, all without readable markings.

### Beat 8

Narration:
A spiral dial predicted eclipses.

Visual:
Tight paper-cut view of one rear spiral dial, with its pointer advancing along the spiral while a stylized dark Moon overlaps an amber Sun above the dial as an eclipse symbol.

### Beat 9

Narration:
Another marked major games, including the Olympics.

Visual:
Paper-cut dial segment beside a symbolic ancient athletic scene: a small laurel wreath, stadium curve, and two simplified Greek runners appear as visual motifs linked to a pointer on the mechanism, with no lettering.

### Beat 10

Narration:
Researchers reconstructed its hidden gearwork.

Visual:
The Red Horned Everyman stands beside a tabletop exploded reconstruction made of layered paper bronze gears suspended in depth, calmly studying how separated wheels align into one dense mechanism.

### Beat 11

Narration:
Its complexity inspired the nickname ancient computer.

Visual:
Dense paper-cut reconstruction fills the frame like an intricate mechanical brain, dozens of interlocking bronze wheels nested inside the case while the outer silhouette remains unmistakably ancient and non-electronic.

### Beat 12

Narration:
It modeled the sky without electricity.

Visual:
Final paper-cut tableau of the complete bronze mechanism below a layered celestial arc of Sun, Moon, stars, and orbital curves, with the Red Horned Everyman quietly observing from the lower edge and no wires, batteries, or modern components.
