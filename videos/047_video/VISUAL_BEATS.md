# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Outside pressure is lower at high altitude.

Visual:
Full-bleed crayon illustration from outside a passenger airplane cruising high above a thick cloud layer, with the aircraft surrounded by thin, sparse-looking high-altitude atmosphere and expansive cool blue sky; no text, labels, borders, panels or inset framing, with the bottom 8-10% kept naturally calm for subtitles.

### Beat 2

Narration:
A break lets cabin air rush out.

Visual:
Inside the aircraft cabin in full-bleed crayon style, a damaged side window is seen from a close three-quarter angle as loose lightweight objects and dense directional crayon airflow strokes stream outward through the opening; passengers remain safely farther back, with no readable text or enclosing frame.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Airflow is strongest near the opening.

Visual:
A tight side-on crayon view of the broken window zone shows the most concentrated, fastest-looking airflow strokes packed immediately beside the opening, with nearby lightweight objects sharply angled toward it and calmer cabin space visible beyond; no labels or printed arrows.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Farther away, the air may move much less.

Visual:
A wide view from the opposite end of the same cabin shows the broken window small in the distant background while foreground passengers, seat fabric and loose objects remain comparatively still; the Newton-Inspired Scholar stands securely in the calm foreground indicating the contrast through posture only, without signs or text.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Pressure falls, making breathing harder.

Visual:
A medium frontal cabin shot shows a seated passenger reacting to the pressure drop with a strained breathing posture while the surrounding cabin gains subtle thinning, expanding crayon atmosphere strokes and cooler tones; the broken window is out of frame so the focus stays on the consequence, with no labels or medical text.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Oxygen masks may drop as pilots descend.

Visual:
Full-bleed upward-angled cabin view with several oxygen masks freshly hanging from the overhead units as passengers reach for them, while through the windows the airplane is visibly descending toward a lower cloud layer and warmer atmosphere; the Newton-Inspired Scholar is seated and correctly using a mask, with no printed instructions.

### Beat 7

Narration:
That sudden pressure loss is called rapid decompression.

Visual:
A final full-bleed crayon cabin scene freezes the overall event into one clear visual: the broken window glows with restrained warm hazard accents, concentrated airflow remains localized near it, oxygen masks hang farther inside, and the aircraft is now lower above a thicker cloud layer outside; no labels, text, borders, cards or footer.
