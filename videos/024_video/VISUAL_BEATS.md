# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
In 1935, Erwin Schrödinger imagined a cat sealed in a box.

Visual:
Surreal cut-paper portrait of 1930s physicist Erwin Schrödinger at a small desk, looking toward a recessed cardboard shadow-box containing the silhouette of a calm cat behind a firmly closed wooden lid, with warm barn-umber outer layers fading into indigo interior shadow.

### Beat 2

Narration:
Inside, a radioactive atom may decay or not.

Visual:
Extreme close view inside the sealed-box world: a simplified graphite-drawn radioactive atom motif floats above an early-20th-century detector, while two translucent torn-paper probability traces diverge around it to suggest decay and non-decay without labels.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Decay triggers a mechanism that releases poison.

Visual:
Side-on technical cut-paper view of a plausible 1930s detector-and-lever apparatus in motion: a triggered relay pulls a mechanical linkage that tips a small stoppered poison vessel, shown abstractly and without any animal in frame.

### Beat 4

Narration:
No decay means the cat survives.

Visual:
Low-angle interior view of the box with a calm alert cat sitting upright in a warm pool of cream light; behind it, the detector and lever remain visibly motionless and the poison vessel stays securely upright.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Quantum theory allows both atomic possibilities in superposition.

Visual:
Abstract overhead collage where two translucent versions of the atom occupy the same central space, one surrounded by a decay-like burst and the other by an intact orbital form, interwoven with restrained wave-like cyan-green traces over layered indigo paper.

### Beat 6

Narration:
That links the cat to both possible fates.

Visual:
Frontal shadow-box composition in which one cat silhouette overlaps two spatially offset states within the same recessed chamber: an upright alert form in warm paper and a quiet recumbent form in smoky indigo, connected back to the dual atom motif by a single branching graphite-and-cyan linkage.

### Beat 7

Narration:
Schrödinger used this absurd scenario to expose the measurement problem.

Visual:
Wide surreal shadow-box scene with Schrödinger standing outside the sealed box, one hand near but not touching its latch, while the box interior remains layered with overlapping cat-state silhouettes that collapse toward a single crisp outline only at the edge of the unopened door.
