Someone cuts in line, then spills coffee five minutes later. “Karma,” you think. But is karma actually a force that pays people back?

In several Indian religious traditions, karma is a much deeper idea: actions have consequences that can shape future experience, sometimes across lifetimes. Science can’t confirm a cosmic system that rewards good behavior and punishes bad behavior.

But there’s a more ordinary version of karma we can observe. How you act changes how people respond to you. Kindness can build trust. Cruelty can damage relationships. Habits also shape what situations you create for yourself.

And our brains are good at noticing satisfying patterns, so when a rude person gets instant payback, it sticks in memory.

So is karma real? As cosmic justice, that’s a matter of belief. As actions creating consequences, it’s happening all the time.
