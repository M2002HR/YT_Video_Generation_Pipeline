# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Eyes catch the stretching jaw.

Visual:
Soft graphite storybook close-up of one observer's eye in sharp pencil focus while, deeper in the frame, another everyday person stretches into a yawn; the yawning mouth is softly reflected in the observer's pupil, with muted sage and straw colored-pencil accents on warm cream paper.

### Beat 2

Narration:
The brain recognizes a familiar human action.

Visual:
Centered graphite side-profile head silhouette on cream paper, with a tiny hand-drawn yawning figure nested inside the softly shaded brain area like a remembered action; sparse broken hatching and muted powder-blue pencil tint keep the concept intuitive rather than medical.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Attention snaps toward it.

Visual:
Over-the-shoulder medium shot in a simple waiting-room setting: a seated person abruptly turns their head toward someone yawning across the room, with two faint graphite motion arcs behind the turning face and peripheral chairs dissolving into unfinished pencil strokes.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Perception and movement systems may prime a matching response.

Visual:
Minimal pencil diagram showing an eye silhouette on the upper left and a jaw-and-neck silhouette on the lower right, connected by a soft chain of ghosted graphite echoes that gradually change from seeing a yawn to preparing the same mouth-opening motion; no labels or interface elements.

### Beat 5

Narration:
That can make your own yawn easier to trigger.

Visual:
Three-quarter close shot of the Red Horned Everyman in a neutral reading nook, calmly trying to suppress a yawn as one hand hovers near his mouth; his jaw has just begun to open, eyes slightly watering, with a faint ghosted wider-open jaw behind him suggesting the response becoming easier.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Scientists call this contagious yawning.

Visual:
Wide cream-paper illustration of a loose semicircle of three ordinary people, one mid-yawn and the other two beginning to mirror the gesture at slightly different stages, their bodies linked only by subtle curved graphite sightlines and repeated open-jaw shapes.

### Beat 7

Narration:
It occurs in humans and some social animals.

Visual:
Soft graphite storybook scene divided spatially without panels: a yawning human pair occupies the lower foreground while, farther up a grassy slope, two non-specific social mammals rest together with one opening its mouth in a yawn; muted moss and pale straw pencil tint unifies the scene.

### Beat 8

Narration:
Social connection may influence who catches it.

Visual:
Top-down graphite illustration of four people around a small café table: one yawns, a close companion beside them begins to yawn too, while two more distant acquaintances remain absorbed elsewhere; subtle proximity arcs and overlapping chair shadows suggest stronger connection without labels.

### Beat 9

Narration:
But the exact mechanism remains unsettled.

Visual:
The Red Horned Everyman stands beside a tabletop covered with loose pencil sketches of an eye, brain silhouette, jaw motion, and paired figures, studying several faint branching graphite paths that approach one another but never resolve into a single connection; his posture is calm and skeptical.

### Beat 10

Narration:
Evidence tying it directly to empathy is mixed.

Visual:
Simple graphite balance-scale metaphor on warm cream paper: one pan holds two closely interacting human silhouettes and the other holds a yawning-face sketch, but the scale sits nearly level and visibly uncertain, surrounded by a few incomplete pencil marks rather than conclusions or labels.

### Beat 11

Narration:
So seeing a yawn can quietly set another in motion.

Visual:
Side-on medium-wide view of two people on opposite benches at a quiet transit stop: the first is finishing a yawn while the second, after glancing over, is just beginning one; a delicate sequence of fading graphite jaw contours bridges the open space between them like a nearly invisible ripple.

### Beat 12

Narration:
One mouth opens, and another suddenly wants to.

Visual:
Return to the opening workshop: the Red Horned Everyman sits beside the worn book and neatly sorted tools, caught in a small involuntary yawn while a second nearby person at the far edge of frame has just opened their mouth first; soft daylight and familiar cream-paper pencil texture close the visual loop.
