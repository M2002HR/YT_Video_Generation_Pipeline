# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Eyes catch the stretching jaw.

Visual:
Soft graphite storybook close-up of the Red Horned Everyman's eyes in three-quarter profile as he notices another person's mouth opening into a yawn at the opposite edge of the cream-paper scene; a faint pencil sightline leads from his gaze to the stretching jaw.

### Beat 2

Narration:
The brain recognizes a familiar human action.

Visual:
A simple graphite anatomical silhouette of a human head seen from the side, with a softly shaded brain shape containing a tiny ghosted pencil sketch of a yawning face, suggesting recognition rather than literal brain detail.

### Beat 3

Narration:
Attention snaps toward it.

Visual:
Overhead storybook view of a small social scene where several muted figures and objects remain softly unfinished while one yawning person is sharply emphasized by converging graphite strokes and the host's head turning toward them.

### Beat 4

Narration:
Perception and movement systems may prime a matching response.

Visual:
Split-free conceptual pencil illustration of a seated human figure with one faint graphite echo tracing from an observed yawning face near the head toward a second ghosted jaw-opening pose in the figure's own body, linked by soft curved motion strokes.

### Beat 5

Narration:
That can make your own yawn easier to trigger.

Visual:
Medium side view of the Red Horned Everyman seated alone at a plain table, lips beginning to part and one hand hovering near his mouth as a barely visible graphite motion echo suggests the yawn is just starting.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Scientists call this contagious yawning.

Visual:
Three people arranged in a loose diagonal across the cream paper, the first fully yawning, the second beginning to open their mouth, and the third just reacting with widened eyes; soft graphite echo-arcs imply the behavior passing along without any labels.

### Beat 7

Narration:
It occurs in humans and some social animals.

Visual:
Gentle storybook scene with a yawning human seated on a low park bench while, several feet away, a non-specific social primate and a domestic dog are each shown in separate natural poses with open mouths suggesting yawns, surrounded by sparse sage foliage.

### Beat 8

Narration:
Social connection may influence who catches it.

Visual:
Two close friends lean toward each other on one side of the frame while two unfamiliar figures remain more distant on the other; a soft pencil ripple from a yawn reaches the close pair clearly but fades before the distant pair.

### Beat 9

Narration:
But the exact mechanism remains unsettled.

Visual:
A restrained graphite research-table still life with overlapping sketches of an eye, brain, jaw, and small human silhouettes connected by several incomplete dotted pencil paths that trail off into unfinished construction marks.

### Beat 10

Narration:
Evidence tying it directly to empathy is mixed.

Visual:
Balanced pencil metaphor of two simple human silhouettes facing a yawning figure: one silhouette has a warm muted brick-red thread-like connection toward the yawner while the other has a broken, fading thread, both surrounded by ambiguous graphite marks rather than symbols or text.

### Beat 11

Narration:
So seeing a yawn can quietly set another in motion.

Visual:
Return to the Red Horned Everyman in a calm three-quarter medium shot as someone in the soft-focus background yawns; the host's expression shifts from skeptical observation to the first involuntary opening of his own mouth, with a subtle graphite motion echo connecting the two actions.
