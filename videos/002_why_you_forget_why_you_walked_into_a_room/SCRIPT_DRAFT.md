You walk into the kitchen, stop, and suddenly realize you have no idea why you came in.

It feels like your brain just deleted the thought. But the doorway may be part of what happened.

Your brain constantly divides experience into little events: sitting at your desk, walking down the hall, entering the kitchen. Crossing into a new space can act like a boundary between those events.

That change of context may make the thought you were holding a little harder to retrieve. It usually isn’t gone. The clues that made it easy to remember have changed.

That’s why walking back to where you started can sometimes bring the thought back almost instantly.

So when you stand in a room wondering why you’re there, your memory may not have failed. Your brain may have simply turned the page before you finished the sentence.
