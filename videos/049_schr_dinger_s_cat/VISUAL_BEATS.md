# Visual Beats

### Beat 1

Narration:
Schrödinger proposed it in 1935.

Visual:
Full-bleed screen-print scene of a period-plausible 1930s physicist's worktable with a closed wooden experiment box, loose scientific apparatus parts, and a human hand arranging the thought-experiment components; no readable writing anywhere.

### Beat 2

Narration:
A cat is sealed in a box.

Visual:
Cut inside the wooden box: a living cat crouches in the enclosed chamber while the heavy lid closes above, rendered as bold screen-print silhouettes with the surrounding enclosure filling the vertical frame.

### Beat 3

Narration:
One radioactive atom may decay.

Visual:
Extreme microscopic visualization of a single radioactive atom suspended against a coarse dark field, with restrained vermilion rupture marks suggesting the possibility of decay without showing a definite outcome.

### Beat 4

Narration:
A detector watches for decay.

Visual:
Period-plausible mechanical radiation detector shown in close profile beside the tiny source chamber, its sensing element aimed toward the atom's container and linked onward by a physical wire.

### Beat 5

Narration:
Decay can trigger a poison mechanism.

Visual:
A vermilion decay impulse reaches the detector and drives a clearly legible mechanical linkage that releases a small hammer toward a sealed poison vial, captured at the instant the trigger begins moving.

### Beat 6

Narration:
Then the cat dies.

Visual:
Wide interior view of the box after the mechanism has fired: the poison vial is broken near the apparatus and the cat lies motionless in deep printed shadow, with the machinery receding behind it.

### Beat 7

Narration:
No decay leaves it alive.

Visual:
A distinctly different high-angle view of the same conceptual box in the no-decay outcome: the poison vial remains intact, the trigger is untouched, and the alert cat stands alive beneath broad pale illumination.

### Beat 8

Narration:
Quantum theory can describe both possibilities together.

Visual:
The box interior is overlaid as two slightly misregistered screen-print states occupying the same full-bleed space: one imprint contains the standing live cat and intact mechanism while the other contains the motionless cat and fired apparatus.

### Beat 9

Narration:
That state is called a superposition.

Visual:
A simplified full-frame conceptual image isolates the overlapping cat states and apparatus as doubled, misregistered silhouettes within one shared box volume, emphasizing coexistence of alternatives without any labels or text.

### Beat 10

Narration:
That jump from atom to cat was the point.

Visual:
Full-bleed scale-bridge composition with a huge magnified radioactive atom dominating the lower foreground and the sealed cat box looming far above in the same continuous space; the Red Horned Everyman stands small between them, observing the visual chain from microscopic source to macroscopic consequence.
