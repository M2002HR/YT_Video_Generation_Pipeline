Episode World Design
Governing Metaphor

A fog-bound Scandinavian shoreline becomes a branching world of routes. The Seeker begins beside one longship, then discovers that the path does not lead back to a single “Viking homeland” or nation; it branches outward from several Scandinavian societies into western seas and eastern river systems. Use branching waterways, coastlines, and settlements—not maps or text—as the visual metaphor for many origins and many kinds of journeys.

The Question Book

A tall, narrow book suited to the 9:16 frame, bound in weathered blue-gray leather with dark timber corner pieces and small brass fittings. The cover emblem is a simple longship beneath three converging northern headlands, avoiding modern flags or national symbols.

Pages are thick, fibrous parchment with faint wave, timber, and braided-border impressions. When opened, the center fold darkens into a narrow channel of seawater. Inked coastlines spread organically across the pages, then lift into miniature cliffs, waves, and a striped sail.

Portal Transition

Fog pours upward from the open book while the illustrated sea gains depth. The Page Key catches cold reflected light and the page gutter stretches into a real waterway.

The Seeker steps toward the rising sail as Library shelves dissolve into vertical shapes resembling cliffs and ship timbers. Complete the transition with the Seeker standing on a Scandinavian shore as the book-page texture disappears beneath wet stone and grass.

Subject World

Create a grounded Viking-Age Scandinavian environment rather than a fantasy “Viking kingdom.”

The core location is a cold coastal inlet surrounded by rocky slopes, pine and birch woodland, timber buildings, working docks, boats, livestock, tools, cargo, and ordinary human activity. Longships are important but should coexist with smaller vessels and settlements so the world represents societies rather than only raiders.

From this home region, exploration expands through two visually distinct route systems: open northern seas leading west, and broad waterways leading east. Suggest distant Britain, North Atlantic islands, Greenlandic coastlines, Baltic waterways, and more distant eastern trade routes through changing geography and architecture, without attempting to show every destination individually.

Keep raiding, trading, exploration, migration, and settlement visually balanced. Warriors may appear where appropriate, but merchants, sailors, craftspeople, families, cargo, farms, and settlements must prevent “Viking” from becoming synonymous with warrior.

Palette, Materials, and Light

Palette: slate blue, sea gray, muted pine green, weathered brown, bone parchment, charcoal, oxidized teal, with restrained amber from lamps, fire, and the Page Key.

Materials: wet timber, wool, linen, rough stone, iron, rope, leather, carved wood, sailcloth, grass, parchment, and aged brass.

Opening light is cool and diffused through heavy sea fog. Scandinavian settlement scenes gain soft overcast daylight and occasional warm hearth light. Travel imagery becomes clearer and more expansive as the routes open. The final Library return restores warm amber lamplight.

Maintain tactile hand-drawn educational-adventure rendering with organic linework, restrained painterly texture, atmospheric depth, and readable silhouettes.

Seeker Adaptation

Preserve the canonical face, dark hair, small brass glasses, teal/moss core clothing, satchel, and brass Page Key.

Adapt roughly 20% of the outfit: add a muted wool outer wrap or short weatherproof cloak, simple woven trim, and practical leather fastening suitable for a cold maritime environment. Keep the silhouette recognizably the Seeker rather than transforming them into a Viking costume.

No horned helmet, exaggerated armor, fantasy fur outfit, or oversized weapons.

Recurring Locations and Props

Use these repeatedly to bind standalone images together:

Scandinavian inlet with a timber landing, rocky shoreline, settlement roofs, and distant wooded slopes.

One recognizable longship with a weathered striped wool sail, carved but restrained prow, rope rigging, shields only when contextually appropriate, and visible working cargo.

The branching shoreline/waterway motif, repeatedly revealing that routes move in multiple directions.

Wooden crates, wool bundles, iron tools, rope, barrels, trade goods, and domestic objects to broaden the visual vocabulary beyond warfare.

The brass Page Key, whose warm glow remains the strongest channel-identity accent.

Subtle book echoes in curved shorelines, sail folds, timber frames, or distant geography without turning the subject world literally into a library.

Visual Arc

Opening: In the Library, the Seeker discovers the tall sea-worn Question Book. Its cover opens into fog and dark water. A striped sail rises from the page, transitioning directly into a longship cutting through a Scandinavian inlet.

Middle: Establish several Scandinavian communities rather than one centralized Viking nation. Follow the Seeker from shore to ship as the environment branches into contrasting journeys: briefly suggest western ocean travel and settlement, then eastern river travel and trade. Keep destinations compressed so the sequence emphasizes the breadth of movement rather than becoming a checklist.

Payoff / return: Pull back from one traveler or ship to reveal several separate Scandinavian shores and communities feeding into branching sea and river routes. The visual reframe is that “Viking” is not represented as a single people emerging from one point, but as an activity-associated identity connected with people leaving different Scandinavian societies for different purposes. The branching routes curl subtly into page-like contours; the Page Key activates, returning the Seeker to the Library beside the now-closed Question Book.

Continuity Rules

Maintain a vertical 9:16 composition with one dominant central subject and generous safe space above and below for every image.

Keep the same longship design, sail pattern, Scandinavian inlet geography, Seeker appearance, clothing adaptation, satchel, and Page Key across beats.

Treat Norway, Denmark, and Sweden as modern geographic references only; do not introduce modern flags, borders, national colors, or a unified Viking state.

Do not imply that everyone in Viking-Age Scandinavia was a Viking or that all voyages were raids. Repeatedly include trade, exploration, migration, settlement, and ordinary community life.

When showing distant routes, favor environmental suggestion over precise map-like geography. Do not visually claim a specific route, settlement, artifact, or cultural interaction beyond what the narration supports.

Keep the world historically grounded but illustrative rather than claiming archaeological reconstruction-level precision.

Use fog, water, branching coastlines, and Page Key light as the primary transition language between beats.

Avoid

Horned helmets; fantasy Vikings; enormous axes as default props; uniformly armored populations; modern Scandinavian flags or borders; a single king or capital representing all Vikings; one homogeneous “Viking nation”; nonstop battle imagery; excessive skulls or brutality; generic medieval castles; anachronistic fantasy architecture; text labels; maps requiring captions; runic writing used as decorative gibberish; magical mechanisms implying unsupported history; grids; comic panels; logos; UI overlays; horizontal compositions that become unreadable in 9:16.
